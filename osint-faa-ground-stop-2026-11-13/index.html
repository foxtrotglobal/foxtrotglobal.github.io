FAA Nationwide Ground Stop — 13 November 2026

Open-Source Intelligence Desk  |  Information cut-off: 8:00 PM CT, Fri 13 Nov 2026  |  Classification: UNCLASSIFIED // OPEN SOURCE  |  Update to the 3:40 PM CT edition

UPDATE: THE NATIONWIDE GROUND STOP HAS BEEN LIFTED. ATCSCC Advisory 005, “CNX NATIONWIDE GROUND STOP,” signed 01:19Z (7:19 PM CT), states: “THE FAA COMMAND CENTER NATIONWIDE GROUND STOP HAS BEEN CANCELLED.” The event time on the advisory is 14/0116Z (7:16 PM CT).[47] Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said at an evening briefing, “At 8:15 tonight, Eastern time, we've opened up the airspace,” and warned that restart would be slow.[51][55] The Washington Post, independently, said the stop ended shortly after 8 p.m. Eastern.[54] The Associated Press reported that departures could resume after the communications-related stop.[53][66] The national order was in effect for 4 hours and 22 minutes, from the 2:54 PM CT signature on Advisory 111 to the 7:16 PM CT cancellation.[1][47][61] Officials attribute the stop to a “temporary telecommunications outage” and to “instability” in radar and communications at en-route centers and TRACONs.[50][51][55] A specific vendor, software change, or final root-cause finding has not been officially named.[49][51]
VERDICT: CONFIRMED, NOW CANCELLED. This was a real nationwide departure stop, not a recycled story. Afternoon confirmation came from the Transportation Secretary, @FAANews and major U.S. outlets.[5][6][11] It was the second all-departures national stop since 11 September 2001, after the January 2023 NOTAM outage.[1][52][53] Departures may resume, but aircraft, crews and gates are out of position, and knock-on delays and cancellations are expected into Saturday.[51][55][61] The mid-afternoon claim that the stop had already been lifted was incorrect; it was a display lag on the public status board, not a cancellation.[4][14][44]

1. Key judgments

  • Confirmed: The stop is cancelled. Advisory 005 covers all destination airports and all facilities and gives no residual reason code.[47] The public NAS Status board, checked at about 7:48 PM CT, no longer lists a national ground stop.[62]
  • Official cause, as stated: a communications / telecommunications problem affecting Air Route Traffic Control Centers, later described by Sec. Duffy as “instability for our radar and our communications in our centers, in our TRACONs throughout the country.” He added, “We didn’t lose all telecom, we didn’t lose all radar,” and said the stop was ordered out of an abundance of caution.[5][49][50][55] Advisory 120, which kept the stop in force at 4:46 PM CT, listed the reason as “COMMUNICATIONS FAILURE” / “EQUIPMENT / OUTAGE.”[48] The FAA has not published a final root-cause report and has not officially named a vendor or a software update.[49]
  • Credible but unofficial reporting (unverified by the FAA):
    • Multiple sources told CBS News that an update to the private communications network that connects the air-traffic-control system caused the system to reboot and crash. CBS said the network is operated by L3Harris, which did not immediately comment.[51]
    • The Wall Street Journal, as reported by The Guardian, said people briefed on the matter described an outage on a telecommunications network overseen by L3Harris that handles controller-to-pilot radio frequencies. It was not immediately clear whether radar feeds were also affected.[59]
    • Earlier, the FAA's message to airlines cited “ATC Frequencies and/or Radar,” and FAA employees described a “rolling frequency outage.”[9][10][45][25]
    These accounts are consistent with the official “telecommunications outage” wording, but they remain unverified until the FAA or the vendor confirms them.
  • Threat assessment (official): Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, speaking at the White House, said there was “no indication as of yet” of a “nefarious act,” that there was no reason to believe it was “a threat to the United States,” and that “everybody is safe.”[56][57][52] He later said, “So far what we’ve seen there is no cyberattack. We’re still working through that process.”[55] In an earlier exchange he would not formally rule a cyberattack out while the investigation continued.[56] No TSA, FBI or NORAD operational statement on the event was found. TSA and FBI accounts carried routine posts during the afternoon stop.[40][41]
  • Most likely explanation (assessed): a failure in the telecommunications or radio/radar network that links ARTCCs and TRACONs to remote sites, now described officially as a temporary outage or instability. A software update on a contractor-run FAA network is a plausible mechanism, but it is not an official finding. We have no open-source evidence that the stop was caused by a confirmed cyberattack, GPS jamming, a staffing shortage, or a security operation against hijackers.

3. Scope

  • Departures: all U.S. airports were held from 2:54 PM CT until 7:16 PM CT, with some selective international releases late in the window.[1][47][48][51]
  • Airborne aircraft: were allowed to continue to their destinations. No order to land at the nearest airport has been reported, a key difference from 9/11.[8][15][32][51] Flightradar24 counted about 3,100 airborne aircraft bound for U.S. airports near the start.[20] Duffy later said there were around 3,500 aircraft in the air when the stop was called and about 400 still airborne when he last checked at the evening briefing.[55] Those figures are official/reported counts, not a single reconciled total.
  • International inbound: handled case by case throughout.[1][50]
  • Facilities named: officials said “all” or “multiple” ARTCCs, and Duffy later included TRACONs.[5][7][55] The first confirmed local stops were at Chicago O'Hare and Midway.[12][13] Unverified afternoon chatter also named Chicago, New York, Boston and Kansas City Centers.[24][43]

On the “extraordinary circumstances” wording: it is the Command Center's catch-all phrase for “by order of the Command Center.” It does not by itself signal a security event. The FAA paired it with a communications cause from the first public statements, and later with a telecommunications outage.[1][7][50]

On the SMART / AI tool: Air Space Intelligence, which makes the FAA's recently discussed SMART traffic-management tool, said the ground stop “is not based on or a result of the SMART system, any product of ASI or our integration with FAA and ATC.”[52] No official has attributed the outage to that tool. Treat any such link as unverified and contradicted by the vendor.

5. Official statements after the lift

  • DOT / FAA: Sec. Duffy said airspace opened at 8:15 PM ET, restart would be slow, and the underlying problem was instability in radar and communications at centers and TRACONs, not a total loss of either.[51][55] The written FAA statement on the agency website still describes a preliminary investigation and has not, as of this cut-off, been replaced with a root-cause report.[49]
  • DHS / White House: Sec. Mullin briefed at the White House, said President Trump had been briefed, attributed the event to a telecommunications outage, said travelers were safe, and later said there was so far no sign of a cyberattack, with work still underway.[56][55][57] A White House video of the afternoon remarks is posted.[57]
  • President Trump: Peter Doocy of Fox News, speaking from the White House, said he had just spoken with the president, who said it did not seem like a cyberattack or AI, that “there’s nobody taking credit,” and that it seemed instead to involve “old broken stuff” in FAA infrastructure, including equipment about 55 years old.[60] This is a reporter's relay of a presidential comment, not a technical report.
  • TSA, FBI, NORAD: No operational statement on this event was found. Afternoon posts from TSA and the FBI were routine and did not address the stop.[40][41]
  • Congress: Senate Commerce Committee Republicans called the disruption rare and major and used it to argue for modernizing en-route centers, noting prior funding of $12.5 billion, including $1.9 billion for facilities, and the administration's request this week for another $30.1 billion.[63][8] Ranking Member Maria Cantwell said the committee will review “how planes communicate through primary frequencies, backup frequencies, and other issues.”[63] Sen. Tammy Duckworth, Aviation Subcommittee ranking member, said “the safety of our skies is built on redundancies” and that she will demand answers from the FAA.[64]

7. Recovery and Saturday knock-on

  • Duffy warned that opening the airspace does not mean every flight will leave immediately: “It's going to be slow.” NBC, citing a source just before the lift, said recovery would take time because crews and aircraft are out of place and because of pilot and flight-attendant duty limits.[51][55]
  • IFR Meter, reading Command Center data, reported that ground-delay programs at LaGuardia, Boston, Las Vegas, San Diego and San Francisco were cancelled within minutes of the national stop, that a United-only ground stop to O'Hare was issued just after the lift, and that New York and Philadelphia departures were under 20-miles-in-trail spacing, in part for staffing. Arrival-metering counts were a small fraction of the same time the day before, then began to climb slowly.[61]
  • United issued a travel waiver during the stop and said cancellations were being made because of congestion; international departures were being managed case by case, and some airborne flights were diverting because of gate congestion.[65] American had already warned of a telecommunications problem at many ATC facilities.[16]
  • At DFW, Love Field, IAH and Hobby, Friday evening banks were heavily cancelled or delayed, as the table in §6 shows.[28][29][30][31][65] Crews timing out and aircraft ending the night in the wrong cities make Saturday morning disruption likely, even with the national stop gone. (Assessment, consistent with official and airline warnings.)
  • Passengers should check the airline app before going to the airport and use published waivers where available. (Advice.)

8. Prior comparable incidents

  • Sept 11, 2001: the Command Center ordered a nationwide ground stop at 9:25 ET, then at 9:42 ordered all aircraft to land. About 4,500 aircraft landed.[32]
  • Jan 11, 2023: a NOTAM system failure caused a domestic departure stop of under 2 hours. It was the only all-departures national stop between 9/11 and today.[8][15][52]
  • May 17, 1995: a computer outage at Chicago Center led to a national ground stop for traffic headed into Chicago airspace.[33]
  • Apr 28, 2025: at Newark/Philadelphia TRACON, a failed telecom line caused a radar and radio blackout of up to 90 seconds.[34][35]
  • Funding context: Congress approved $12.5B for ATC communications last year. The FAA and White House asked this week for another $30.1B. Lawmakers on both sides have already tied today's outage to that modernization debate.[8][63]

9. What remains open

  • A published FAA or DOT root-cause statement that names the failed system, any vendor (including whether L3Harris's network was involved), and whether a software update was the trigger.
  • The close-out of the DHS/FAA investigation on cyber, and any statement from CISA, the FBI or NORAD, if one is issued.
  • Saturday cancellation and delay totals, especially at DFW, DAL, IAH and HOU, once overnight aircraft and crew repositioning is visible.
  • Any NATCA statement on what controllers could and could not see or hear during the outage.
